\opening{Dear Editor,}

Please consider the enclosed manuscript ``Neural Pre-Decoding Under Device Calibrated Noise: IBM Calibration Data,
Correlated Matching and Integer Inference on One Alveo U55C'' for publication in The Journal of Supercomputing.

Neural pre-decoders for the surface code are trained and judged on uniform noise and against plain matching. The
manuscript evaluates NVIDIA's published pre-decoder on circuits whose every fault location carries the error rate of
a real IBM qubit or coupler, taken from calibration files in CSV form, over \NumPoints{} experimental points on eight
devices and \TotalShots{} shots. It reports a result that matters for anyone building decoding hardware: the network
beats plain matching at most points, but PyMatching with correlated matching, which needs no network, beats the
network at every distance 5 point. It also shows that calibrated matching weights improve matching by up to
\MedWeightsMax{} times on their own, and that the network loses its advantage on strongly uneven qubits. The integer
network runs on one Alveo U55C card, bit exact on every run and identical to the CPU integer model in every logical
outcome wherever both were run; the manuscript states openly that a large CPU is faster for this network.

The FPGA kernel and bitstream are those of the author's earlier four card throughput study, whose code is public
(github.com/nasir26/u55c-neural-predecoder-nodes); the present manuscript reuses that kernel but reports no result
from that study. Every number in the manuscript is generated by script from the stored per shot records, and every
reference was checked against Crossref or arXiv, apart from vendor and software pages, which were checked at their
sources. The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. Thank you for your consideration.

\closing{Sincerely,}
